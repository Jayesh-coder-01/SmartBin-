# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional requirements, non-functional requirements, system architecture, data design, interfaces, workflows, constraints, and acceptance criteria for the SmartBin minimum viable product (MVP). It is the shared reference for implementation, testing, demonstration, and future maintenance.

## 1.2 Project Purpose

SmartBin is a location-based waste collection management system for a designated locality. It enables supervisors to register public waste bins, manage workers and teams, allocate bin responsibilities, and monitor reported conditions and collection activities. Workers identify bins through unique QR codes, submit before-and-after photographs, review AI-assisted condition reports, and record collection activities.

The system stores inspection and collection history so supervisors can review activity over time. AI analysis estimates visible bin conditions; it does not independently verify that waste was collected or that cleaning was completed.

## 1.3 Background

Waste collection operations require bin-location records, clear responsibility assignments, condition checks, and records of completed activities. When these details are managed through disconnected or manual processes, supervisors may have limited visibility into the condition and history of individual bins. SmartBin brings bin identification, assignments, photo evidence, AI-assisted condition reporting, and collection records into one application.

## 1.4 Problem Statement

Monitoring individual public waste bins and coordinating collection can be difficult when supervisors cannot easily see each bin’s location, latest reported condition, current responsibility, and previous collection activity. A centralized locality-level system is required to support bin registration, assignment management, QR-based identification, photo-based inspection, condition reporting, and traceable collection records.

## 1.5 Objectives

1. Maintain a centralized registry of public waste bins and their geographic locations.

2. Assign individual bins or groups of bins to workers or teams, including temporary reassignment.

3. Display bins on an interactive map and in role-appropriate lists.

4. Generate and scan unique QR codes for bin identification.

5. Capture before-and-after photographs for inspection and collection workflows.

6. Use an AI service to estimate visible fill level, overflow, and surrounding scattered waste.

7. Preserve inspection, correction, assignment, and collection history.

8. Notify relevant users about assignments, emergency reports, and overdue inspections.

9. Provide supervisors with locality-level monitoring and workers with assigned-bin information.

10. Provide a role-restricted, read-only chatbot grounded in authorized SmartBin records.

## 1.6 Scope

### Included in the MVP

One designated locality managed by one authorized supervisor context.

Two primary roles: Supervisor and Sanitation Worker.

Bin registration, editing, deactivation/reactivation, GPS coordinates, map display, and QR generation.

Worker and team management, permanent and temporary assignments, assignment acceptance/rejection, and assignment history.

QR scanning with manual bin-code fallback.

Normal inspections and collections with before-and-after photographs.

Gemini-based image analysis and before/after visual comparison.

Worker confirmation/correction and supervisor review/correction of AI reports.

Emergency reports for registered bins outside the reporting worker’s assignment.

Inspection scheduling, overdue detection, manual priority, and in-app notifications.

Hindi and English interface support.

A read-only, database-connected chatbot for workers and supervisors.

Ninety-day photo retention, followed by image deletion while operational history remains.

### Excluded from the MVP

City-wide or multi-locality operations.

Live vehicle GPS tracking, vehicle dispatch, and route optimization.

IoT fill-level sensors or direct sensor-based measurements.

Citizen/public complaint portals and municipal-system integrations.

Worker attendance, payroll, salary, or performance-ranking systems.

Offline synchronization.

SMS, email, or push notifications for operational alerts; operational notifications are in-app only.

Chatbot actions that create, update, or delete application records.

Automatic replacement-worker selection after an assignment rejection.

# 2. Overall System Description

## 2.1 Product Perspective

SmartBin is a responsive web application with separate worker and supervisor experiences. The frontend communicates with Supabase services for authentication, database access, image storage, and secure backend processing. Supabase Edge Functions call the Gemini API for image analysis and chatbot responses. Leaflet and OpenStreetMap provide the map interface.

## 2.2 Major System Capabilities

User authentication, role-based access, and secure worker invitations.

Bin registry, locality map, bin status, QR generation, and manual bin-code lookup.

Worker/team management with historical team membership.

Permanent and temporary bin assignments, group assignment batches, acceptance/rejection, and restoration rules.

Normal QR-based inspection and collection workflows with image uploads.

AI-based estimates of fill percentage, visible overflow, surrounding waste, and confidence.

Separate storage of original AI results, worker review, and supervisor review.

Emergency reporting for unassigned registered bins without granting normal task permissions.

Inspection due-date calculation, overdue alerts, and manual high-priority flags.

Role-specific maps, dashboards, activity history, and in-app notifications.

Read-only chatbot responses based on authorized database query results.

## 2.3 User Classes

## 2.4 Operating Environment

Responsive web application accessed through modern desktop and mobile browsers.

React with Vite for the frontend and Tailwind CSS for styling.

Supabase Auth, PostgreSQL, Storage, and Edge Functions for backend services.

Google Gemini API for image analysis and chatbot responses.

Leaflet with OpenStreetMap map data.

QRCode library for QR generation and html5-qrcode for browser-based scanning.

Vercel for frontend deployment and GitHub for version control.

Active internet is required for authentication, database operations, image uploads, and AI processing.

## 2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints

The MVP is limited to one locality and two primary application roles.

The Gemini API key and Supabase service-role credentials must remain in backend secrets and must never be bundled into frontend code.

All protected data access must be enforced by backend authorization and Supabase RLS, not only by hiding interface controls.

A normal inspection or collection requires a current assignment; emergency reporting is a separate, limited workflow.

Normal collection completion requires a successfully uploaded after photograph. A no-photo exception is a supervisor-recorded exception, not a normal completed collection.

AI results are estimates of visible image content and must not be represented as exact measurements or proof of collection.

Photos are deleted after 90 days; operational records and analysis results remain.

## 2.6 Assumptions and Dependencies

A supervisor is available to configure the locality, inspection interval, bins, workers, and teams.

Workers have compatible smartphones with camera access and a supported browser.

Supervisors provide accurate bin identifiers, capacity information, and geographic locations.

Workers capture photographs that reasonably show the bin and relevant surroundings.

Gemini API access, Supabase services, map tiles, and internet connectivity are available when used.

A registered bin has a unique QR identifier; manual bin-code entry is available if scanning fails.

# 3. User Characteristics and Permissions

## 3.1 Role Definitions

### Sanitation Worker

A worker uses the mobile-oriented interface to view bins currently assigned individually or through the worker’s active team, review relevant history, acknowledge assignments, scan QR codes, submit inspection and collection photographs, review AI reports, and see personal activity. A worker may submit an emergency report for any registered bin but may not use that report to perform normal collection on an unassigned bin.

### Supervisor

A supervisor manages bins, worker accounts, teams, assignments, priorities, and the locality inspection schedule. The supervisor can view all bins and complete inspection and collection history within the supervisor’s authorized locality, review emergency reports, and enter separately auditable corrections to AI reports.

## 3.2 Permission Matrix

## 3.3 Access and Membership Rules

Each worker belongs to no more than one active team at a time. Historical team memberships are retained using joined_at and left_at timestamps.

A team assignment is visible to active members of that team. Any active team member may perform a team-assigned task after the team assignment has been accepted.

The worker ID recorded on each inspection, collection, or emergency report must identify the individual who actually performed that action.

Only one current assignment may control a bin at a time. A temporary assignment suspends, but does not delete, the permanent assignment it replaces.

Workers can view history for bins currently assigned to them, including relevant earlier records for those bins. Removing an assignment removes ongoing bin access, but does not delete the worker’s own activity records.

Supervisors can access only the locality or area authorized for their account. The MVP is configured for one locality.

Backend checks and RLS policies enforce these rules. Frontend route guards and hidden buttons are supplementary only.

# 4. Functional Requirements

The word “shall” indicates a mandatory requirement for the MVP. Each requirement is intended to be testable.

### FR-01 — Authentication and Account Management

The system shall authenticate workers and supervisors through Supabase Auth.

The system shall retrieve the authenticated user’s role, active status, and authorized area from the profile record.

The initial supervisor account shall be configured securely during system setup.

Supervisors shall create worker accounts through a secure invitation flow. Workers shall set their own passwords; supervisors shall not create or know worker passwords.

The system shall deny access to inactive accounts and shall prevent workers from accessing supervisor-only functions.

### FR-02 — Locality and Initial Configuration

The system shall maintain one locality record for the MVP.

The supervisor shall configure one common inspection interval in hours for all active bins in the locality.

The system shall store the configured interval and use it when calculating each active bin’s next inspection due time.

### FR-03 — Bin Registration and Management

Supervisors shall register bins with a unique bin code, bin type, capacity, area, latitude, longitude, QR identifier, and active status.

The system shall validate required fields, coordinate ranges, positive capacity, and uniqueness of bin code and QR identifier.

Supervisors shall edit permitted bin details and generate, preview, download, or print a QR code.

Supervisors shall be able to deactivate and reactivate bins. Deactivated bins shall not receive new assignments or normal activities.

When a bin is deactivated, current assignments shall be ended and suspended assignments shall not be automatically restored. Historical records shall remain available to supervisors.

### FR-04 — Map, List, and Bin Details

The system shall display registered bins on an interactive map using their stored coordinates.

Workers shall see only bins currently assigned to them individually or through their active team.

Supervisors shall see all bins in their authorized locality.

Selecting a bin shall display its identifier, type, location, latest condition report, current assignment, latest inspection, latest collection record, and permitted history.

The worker interface shall default to a list view on small screens and provide a map toggle. The supervisor interface shall show map and list together on wider screens where practical.

### FR-05 — Worker and Team Management

Supervisors shall create, edit, deactivate, and reactivate worker accounts and teams.

Supervisors shall add workers to teams and move workers between teams.

The system shall preserve team membership history and enforce no more than one active team membership per worker.

Only active workers and active teams with active members shall receive new assignments.

### FR-06 — Permanent and Temporary Assignment Management

Supervisors shall assign one bin or a group of bins to either one worker or one team; an assignment record shall never target both a worker and a team.

The system shall store one bin-specific assignment record per bin. Records created as one bulk action shall share a batch_id.

The system shall enforce no more than one current assignment per bin.

Each assignment shall have an acceptance status of Pending, Accepted, or Rejected and an assignment state of Current, Suspended, or Ended. End reason shall be stored separately.

An assigned worker, or an active member acting for an assigned team, shall be able to accept or reject the assignment. Rejection shall require a reason and notify the supervisor.

When one active team member accepts a team assignment, the acceptance applies to the team assignment. Any active team member may perform the work, and the actual performer shall be recorded for every activity.

When a temporary assignment replaces a permanent assignment, the permanent assignment shall be marked Suspended and the temporary assignment shall become the Current assignment.

If a temporary assignment is accepted and later completed or cancelled, the system shall end the temporary assignment and restore the suspended permanent assignment transactionally, provided the bin remains active and no replacement assignment is current.

If a temporary assignment is rejected, it shall be ended with the rejection reason. The suspended permanent assignment shall remain suspended until the supervisor manually creates a replacement assignment or explicitly restores the permanent assignment.

If no permanent assignment exists, ending a temporary assignment shall leave the bin unassigned.

Assignment status changes and timestamps shall be retained; assignment records shall not be deleted to represent reassignment.

The dashboard shall show Pending, Accepted, In Progress, Completed, Rejected, and Cancelled views. In Progress shall be derived from linked inspection/collection activity; it is not a separate assignment acceptance status.

### FR-07 — QR Scanning and Authorization

Workers shall scan a bin QR code using a supported smartphone browser camera.

The system shall resolve the QR value to a registered bin and verify bin status, authenticated identity, current assignment, and team membership before allowing normal inspection or collection.

A worker shall be able to enter the human-readable bin code manually if QR scanning fails.

A QR scan shall not bypass backend authorization. Deactivated bins shall not accept new normal activities.

### FR-08 — Emergency Reporting

Any authenticated worker shall be able to submit an emergency report for any active registered bin, including a bin outside the worker’s current assignment.

An emergency report shall require a successfully uploaded photograph and a non-empty description.

Emergency reporting shall create a separate emergency report record and notify the supervisor in-app.

An emergency report shall not grant permission to start a normal inspection, submit a collection record, or modify an assignment for that bin.

The supervisor shall be able to review the report and may create a temporary assignment in response.

Emergency reports shall have a review status and retain reporter identity, bin ID, photograph metadata, description, and timestamps.

### FR-09 — Inspection and Before-Photo Submission

A worker shall start a normal inspection only when authorized by a current accepted assignment for the bin or active team.

The worker shall capture or select a before-cleaning photograph and preview it before upload.

The system shall upload the photograph to Supabase Storage and shall create or finalize the inspection record only after the backend confirms successful storage.

Each inspection shall record bin ID, assignment ID, performing worker ID, before-photo metadata, timestamp, and AI analysis state.

Multiple inspections per bin per day shall be permitted.

A successfully uploaded photograph shall remain stored even if AI analysis fails.

### FR-10 — AI Image Analysis

The backend shall submit the uploaded before photograph to Gemini through a Supabase Edge Function.

The AI shall return a structured result containing estimated fill percentage, fill category, visible overflow, surrounding scattered waste, confidence score or indicator, and analysis status.

The fill category shall be derived from the estimated percentage using these intervals: Empty (0–10%), Low (>10–30%), Medium (>30–60%), High (>60–90%), and Very High (>90–100%).

Visible overflow shall be a separate boolean assessment and shall not be inferred solely from the fill category.

Analysis status shall be Completed, Needs Review, or Failed. Needs Review shall represent an unsuitable or uncertain image; Failed shall represent a processing or service failure.

The system shall validate the response structure and allowed values before storing it. Invalid or missing AI fields shall not be presented as confirmed measurements.

The worker shall be able to view the result and confirm or correct it. AI output shall be labelled as an estimate.

### FR-11 — AI Review and Correction History

The system shall preserve the original AI response without overwriting it when a worker or supervisor reviews the result.

Workers shall be able to confirm an AI report or submit corrected values for their own inspection or collection activity.

Supervisors shall be able to review original photographs, original AI results, worker review, and previous correction history.

Supervisors shall be able to enter a separate supervisor correction. Each correction or confirmation shall record reviewer ID, role, action, values, and timestamp.

A later correction shall create another review-history entry rather than overwrite an earlier review entry.

### FR-12 — Collection and After-Photo Workflow

A collection activity shall be linked to one inspection for the same bin. Each inspection may be linked to no more than one collection record.

Multiple collection activities per bin per day shall be permitted by creating separate inspection and collection records.

The worker shall capture and upload an after-cleaning photograph before a normal collection can be marked Completed.

The backend shall analyse the after photograph and compare it with the associated before inspection when possible.

The comparison shall show changes in estimated fill percentage, visible overflow, and surrounding waste. It shall be described as visual comparison only, not proof of collection.

If after-photo AI analysis fails, the collection may still be completed after successful after-photo upload; the AI comparison shall be marked Failed.

If an after photograph cannot be captured or uploaded, normal completion shall be blocked. A supervisor may record an Exception with a reason; an Exception shall not be represented as a normal completed collection.

The individual worker who performed the collection shall be recorded, including when the assignment belongs to a team.

### FR-13 — Inspection Scheduling, Overdue Status, and Priority

The supervisor shall configure one common inspection interval in hours for the locality.

Each active bin shall have a next_inspection_due_at timestamp. The system shall calculate the initial due time at registration or activation.

A successful inspection submission shall update the bin’s next inspection due time using the configured interval, whether AI analysis completes or fails.

A scheduled backend process shall identify active bins whose due time has passed without a newer inspection.

Deactivated bins shall be excluded from overdue detection.

Supervisors shall be able to set a manual priority independently of overdue status. Manual priority and overdue state may both apply to the same bin.

If a bin has a current assignment, overdue notifications shall target the currently assigned worker or active team members and the supervisor. If it has no current assignment, the supervisor shall be notified.

### FR-14 — In-App Notifications

The system shall create in-app notifications for new assignments, assignment rejection, relevant assignment changes, emergency reports, and overdue inspections.

Assignment notifications shall include bin ID, location, assignment type, priority where applicable, and relevant details.

Team assignment notifications shall be delivered to each active team member individually.

Users shall be able to view and mark notifications as read. The system shall retain creation and read timestamps.

Repeated overdue notifications for the same overdue event shall be deduplicated or rate-limited.

Operational SMS, email, and push notifications are outside the MVP.

### FR-15 — Role-Specific Dashboards and History

The worker dashboard shall show current assignments, pending assignment responses, overdue alerts for assigned bins, QR scanner access, recent personal activities, and permitted bin history.

The supervisor dashboard shall show all locality bins, latest reported condition, assignment state, overdue state, manual priority, recent activities, and filters for condition, assignment, and priority.

Selecting a bin shall display bin details and permitted history. Available actions shall depend on role and backend authorization.

Historical inspection and collection records shall remain available after reassignment or bin deactivation, subject to the user’s permissions and photo-retention policy.

### FR-16 — Read-Only AI Chatbot

The system shall provide a chatbot to authenticated workers and supervisors through a secure backend function.

Worker queries shall be restricted to the worker’s current assignments, permitted history, personal activities, and operational instructions.

Supervisor queries shall be restricted to bins, users, assignments, and activities within the supervisor’s authorized locality.

The backend shall retrieve authorized database results using the authenticated identity and role before asking Gemini to formulate a response.

Gemini shall not receive unrestricted database credentials or direct database write access. The chatbot shall not generate or execute unrestricted SQL.

The chatbot shall be read-only and shall not create, update, delete, deactivate, or reassign application records.

If data is missing or unavailable, the chatbot shall state that it cannot retrieve the information rather than inventing an answer.

The chatbot interface and responses shall support Hindi and English and shall handle API, database, empty-input, and invalid-request failures gracefully.

### FR-17 — Photograph Retention and Deletion

Inspection, collection, and emergency photographs shall be retained for 90 days from upload.

A scheduled backend process shall delete expired image objects from Supabase Storage.

After deletion, the associated storage path shall be cleared or marked unavailable and the deleted timestamp shall be recorded.

Operational records, AI results, correction history, worker identities, descriptions, and timestamps shall remain available after image deletion.

The deletion process shall be retryable and shall not delete non-expired photographs.

# 5. Non-Functional Requirements

## 5.1 Usability and Accessibility

The interface shall support Hindi and English, including navigation, action labels, notifications, instructions, error messages, and chatbot presentation.

The selected language shall be retained across subsequent visits for the same user/device context.

The worker interface shall be mobile-first, use large touch targets and recognizable icons, and minimize typing.

Common worker tasks shall be accessible through a small number of clear steps.

The application shall communicate upload progress, AI processing state, success, and failure clearly.

## 5.2 Performance

Normal navigation and dashboard data operations should respond within 3 seconds under typical test conditions, excluding external map tile loading and large file transfer time.

Image uploads shall display progress and shall not be reported as successful until storage confirms the upload.

AI processing shall display a loading state and shall not block access to other permitted application functions.

The system shall paginate or limit long activity histories rather than loading unlimited records in one request.

## 5.3 Security and Privacy

Authentication shall be required for protected application features.

Supabase RLS and backend authorization shall enforce role, area, current assignment, and team membership rules.

The frontend shall never contain Gemini API keys or Supabase service-role credentials.

Privileged account creation, assignment transitions, retention deletion, and other sensitive operations shall be performed through protected backend functions.

Storage access shall be restricted. Photo access shall be checked against the same role and record permissions as the associated operational record.

Chatbot responses shall use only data authorized for the authenticated user and shall not expose secrets or unrestricted database content.

## 5.4 Reliability and Error Handling

The system shall not mark an operation successful until the backend confirms the required record or file has been saved.

A Gemini failure shall not delete or invalidate an already uploaded photograph.

Failed uploads and interrupted submissions shall provide clear retry paths and shall not create duplicate completed records.

Backend operations that change assignment ownership or restore a permanent assignment shall be transactional.

Duplicate task completion shall be prevented through database constraints and transaction-safe backend processing.

## 5.5 Data Integrity and Auditability

Every bin shall have a unique bin code and QR identifier.

Every normal inspection and collection shall identify the bin, assignment, performing worker, and timestamp.

Original AI results shall not be overwritten by worker or supervisor corrections.

Assignment history, team membership history, and emergency report history shall be retained.

Deletion of expired photos shall not delete operational records or AI review history.

Database constraints shall enforce mutually exclusive worker/team assignment targets and unique current assignment per bin.

## 5.6 Maintainability

The application shall separate frontend components, backend functions, database access, AI integration, and storage operations.

Shared validation and authorization rules shall be implemented in reusable backend logic where practical.

The source code shall be maintained in GitHub with a README describing setup, environment variables, deployment, and main workflows.

Secrets shall be stored in environment configuration and excluded from source control.

## 5.7 Compatibility

The web application shall support current versions of major modern browsers used on desktop and smartphones.

QR scanning shall require a supported browser camera and user permission.

The layout shall adapt to desktop, tablet, and smartphone screen widths.

## 5.8 Network Connectivity

The MVP shall require an active internet connection for authentication, database access, uploads, map tiles, and AI requests.

Offline capture and synchronization are outside the MVP.

The interface shall display connectivity or service errors and shall not falsely confirm an uncommitted operation.

## 5.9 AI Output Limitations

AI fill percentages and visual classifications shall be presented as estimates, not sensor measurements.

Visible overflow and surrounding waste shall be reported independently of fill category.

The system shall indicate uncertain or failed analysis and allow human review.

Neither an AI report nor a before/after comparison shall be described as independent proof that waste was collected.

# 6. System Architecture

## 6.1 Architecture Overview

SmartBin uses a web client and managed backend services. The React frontend handles user interaction, map and list presentation, QR scanning, camera capture, and display of results. Supabase provides authentication, PostgreSQL, image storage, and Edge Functions. Edge Functions validate the authenticated request, apply authorization rules, and securely call Gemini. The browser never calls Gemini using a client-exposed secret.

## 6.2 Main Components

### Worker interface

Shows assigned bins, pending assignment responses, overdue alerts, scanner, inspection/collection workflows, recent activities, language switch, and chatbot.

### Supervisor dashboard

Shows locality bins, map/list views, filters, bin management, workers/teams, assignments, emergency reports, overdue status, AI review, and chatbot.

### Authentication and authorization

Uses Supabase Auth for identity and backend/RLS rules for user role, area, assignment, team, and record access.

### Operational database

Stores structured records and preserves history for assignments, inspections, collections, corrections, and notifications.

### Image storage

Stores uploaded images under restricted access and supports the 90-day retention process.

### AI processing

Edge Functions send permitted photos or authorized query results to Gemini and validate structured responses before persistence.

### Scheduling

A scheduled backend process checks inspection due times, creates deduplicated overdue notifications, and deletes expired photographs.

## 6.3 Before-Photo Analysis Data Flow

1. The worker scans the bin QR code or enters the bin code manually.

2. The backend verifies the bin is active and the worker has a current accepted assignment.

3. The worker captures a before photograph and uploads it to Supabase Storage.

4. After storage confirms the upload, the system creates the inspection record and invokes the analysis Edge Function.

5. The Edge Function calls Gemini using a backend secret and requests a defined structured response.

6. The backend validates and stores the original AI result and analysis status.

7. The worker reviews and confirms or corrects the report; the review action is stored separately.

8. The updated report is visible to authorized supervisors.

## 6.4 After-Photo and Collection Data Flow

1. The worker performs the assigned activity and captures the after photograph.

2. The photograph is uploaded to Supabase Storage; normal completion remains blocked until upload succeeds.

3. The backend sends the after photograph to Gemini for analysis when the service is available.

4. The system compares the after result with the linked inspection result when both are available.

5. If after-photo AI analysis fails, the photo remains stored and normal collection may still be completed.

6. The collection log records the performing worker and successful upload. A supervisor-only exception path is available when a photo cannot be captured.

## 6.5 Chatbot Data Flow

1. The authenticated user submits a question.

2. The Edge Function identifies the user and role from the verified session; it does not trust role information supplied in the question.

3. The backend selects an approved query function and applies worker or supervisor access rules.

4. Only the authorized query results and the question are sent to Gemini to formulate a response.

5. The backend returns the response and, where appropriate, the data scope or timestamp used.

6. The chatbot does not execute database writes or unrestricted model-generated SQL.

## 6.6 Security Design

All privileged operations shall validate the current authenticated identity and role on the backend.

RLS shall restrict direct database reads and permitted writes.

Edge Functions shall use server-side secrets and narrowly scoped service access only when required.

Storage objects shall not be publicly readable unless a deliberate, reviewed policy requires it; default access is restricted.

Assignment acceptance, rejection, temporary suspension/restoration, and collection completion shall use transactional backend operations.

## 6.7 System Constraints

The system is online-only in the MVP.

AI image analysis and chatbot responses depend on Gemini API availability and limits.

Map display depends on Leaflet and OpenStreetMap tile availability.

The initial release is limited to a single locality and a small number of roles.

# 7. Database Design

## 7.1 Database Overview and Conventions

Supabase PostgreSQL is the system of record. The schema below is the consolidated MVP design. UUID primary keys are used for internal records. Timestamps use TIMESTAMPTZ and are stored consistently; the interface displays them in the locality’s configured time zone. Foreign keys and check constraints shall enforce the rules that can be represented in the database. More complex authorization and state transitions shall be implemented in protected backend functions and transactions.

The design uses one assignment row per bin. A group assignment is represented by multiple bin-specific rows with the same batch_id. This avoids mixing assignment-batch metadata with the individual bin relationship and avoids the incomplete dual design of a separate assignment_bins table plus bin_id in assignments.

### 7.2.6 assignments

Stores one assignment record per bin. Bulk assignments create multiple rows sharing a batch_id.

Required constraints and transition rules:

Exactly one of assigned_worker_id and assigned_team_id must be non-NULL.

The assigned worker or team must be active and belong to the same area as the bin.

## 7.3 Relationships

One area contains multiple profiles, teams, and bins.

One team has multiple historical membership rows; one worker may have only one active team membership.

One bin has multiple historical assignment rows, inspection records, collection records, and emergency reports.

One bin-specific assignment can be grouped with other assignments using batch_id.

One temporary assignment may reference the permanent assignment it suspends.

One inspection may have zero or one linked collection log. Each collection log refers to one inspection.

One inspection or collection log may have multiple AI review history rows.

One user may receive multiple notifications.

## 7.4 Critical Database Rules and Indexes

Unique constraints: bins.bin_code, bins.qr_code_value, and one current assignment per bin.

Check constraints: positive capacity, valid latitude/longitude, fill percentage between 0 and 100, valid enumerated statuses, and exactly one assignment target (worker XOR team).

Partial unique index: team_members.worker_id where left_at IS NULL.

Partial unique index: assignments.bin_id where assignment_state = Current.

Unique constraint: collection_logs.inspection_id.

Unique constraint: notifications(user_id, event_key) to prevent duplicate event notifications.

Index foreign keys and commonly filtered columns, including area_id, bin_id, assignment status/state, next_inspection_due_at, and created_at.

## 7.5 Access Control and RLS

Workers may read bins for which they have a current assignment through an individual assignment or active team membership.

Workers may read their own operational records and permitted history for currently assigned bins.

Workers may create inspections and collection records only when backend checks confirm an accepted current assignment and active bin.

Any authenticated worker may create an emergency report for an active registered bin, subject to photo and description validation.

Supervisors may read and manage records within their authorized area.

Only supervisors may create or change bins, workers, teams, assignments, priority, inspection schedule, supervisor corrections, and exception records.

RLS shall be enabled for all application tables. Privileged Edge Functions shall validate the user and requested operation before using elevated access.

## 7.6 Photo Retention and Record Preservation

The retention clock starts at each image’s uploaded_at timestamp. At 90 days, the scheduled backend process deletes the corresponding object from Storage. After confirmed deletion, the database path is cleared and the deleted_at field is set. If deletion fails, the job records or logs the failure and retries; it must not clear the path before the object is deleted. Inspection, collection, emergency report details, AI results, correction history, identities, and timestamps remain in PostgreSQL.

## 7.7 Transaction-Safe Assignment and Collection Operations

Creating a temporary assignment shall suspend the referenced permanent assignment and create the temporary current assignment in one transaction.

Accepting or rejecting a bulk assignment batch shall update all bin rows in that batch consistently.

Completing or cancelling a temporary assignment shall end it and restore the suspended permanent assignment atomically when restoration rules permit.

Rejecting a temporary assignment shall not silently restore the permanent assignment; the supervisor must manually select a replacement or explicitly restore the permanent assignment.

Starting or completing a team task shall use a transaction or unique constraints to prevent two workers from completing the same collection record.

# 9. Use Cases and System Workflows

## 9.1 Actors

## 9.2 WF-01 — Login and Account Setup

1. The initial supervisor account is securely configured during system setup.

2. The supervisor signs in and opens the supervisor dashboard.

3. The supervisor creates a worker invitation through the protected account-management flow.

4. The worker receives the invitation and sets their own password.

5. Supabase Auth authenticates the user; the backend retrieves role, active state, and area.

6. The application opens the correct role-specific dashboard.

Alternative cases: invalid credentials are rejected; inactive accounts are denied; expired invitations require a new invitation; unauthorized requests are rejected by the backend.

## 9.3 WF-02 — Bin Registration and QR Generation

1. Supervisor opens Bin Management and selects Register Bin.

2. Supervisor enters bin code, type, capacity, and selects the locality location on the map or uses device GPS.

3. The system validates required fields, coordinates, and uniqueness.

4. The backend saves the bin and generates or assigns a unique QR value.

5. The supervisor previews, downloads, or prints the QR code.

Alternative cases: duplicate bin code/QR is rejected; invalid coordinates require correction; denied GPS permission falls back to manual map selection; QR generation failure does not erase a successfully saved bin.

## 9.4 WF-03 — Worker and Team Management

1. Supervisor creates workers and teams.

2. Supervisor adds active workers to teams.

3. When moving a worker, the system closes the previous team membership and creates a new membership record.

4. The system prevents a worker from having two simultaneous active team memberships.

5. The system retains prior membership history.

## 9.5 WF-04 — Permanent Assignment

1. Supervisor selects one or more bins and chooses one worker or one team.

2. The system creates one bin-specific assignment row per selected bin, sharing a batch_id.

3. The backend verifies bin, worker/team, and area eligibility and enforces one current assignment per bin.

4. The assigned worker or team members receive in-app notifications.

5. The worker or one active team member acting for the team accepts or rejects the assignment.

6. A rejection requires a reason, ends the rejected assignment, and notifies the supervisor.

7. The supervisor manually creates a replacement assignment when necessary.

## 9.6 WF-05 — Temporary Assignment and Restoration

1. Supervisor identifies a bin requiring temporary attention and selects a worker or team.

2. The system suspends the current permanent assignment, if one exists.

3. The system creates a temporary current assignment with Pending acceptance and stores a reference to the permanent assignment it replaces.

4. The assigned worker or one active team member accepts or rejects the temporary assignment.

5. If accepted, the worker/team performs the task. Completion or supervisor cancellation ends the temporary assignment and transactionally restores the suspended permanent assignment, if the bin is active.

6. If rejected, the temporary assignment is ended with the reason. The permanent assignment remains suspended until the supervisor manually reassigns the bin or explicitly restores it.

7. All assignment records and relevant timestamps remain in history.

## 9.7 WF-06 — QR Verification and Emergency Report

### Normal bin access

1. Worker scans a QR code or enters the bin code manually.

2. Backend identifies the bin and validates active status and current accepted assignment.

3. If authorized, the worker opens bin details and selects Start Inspection.

4. If not authorized, normal inspection and collection actions are denied.

### Emergency reporting

1. Worker identifies an overflowing or neglected active registered bin outside their assignment.

2. Worker scans the QR code or enters the bin code.

3. Worker selects Report Emergency, captures and uploads a photograph, and enters a description.

4. Backend saves the emergency report and notifies the supervisor.

5. Supervisor reviews the report and may create a temporary assignment.

6. The emergency report does not grant the worker normal inspection or collection permission.

## 9.8 WF-07 — Before-Photo Inspection and AI Analysis

1. Worker opens an assigned bin and selects Start Inspection.

2. Worker captures and previews the before photograph.

3. Frontend uploads the photograph; backend confirms successful Storage upload.

4. Backend creates the inspection record and calls Gemini through an Edge Function.

5. Gemini returns the structured visible-condition estimate.

6. Backend validates and stores the original AI result and analysis status.

7. Worker confirms or corrects the report; the review is stored separately.

8. The supervisor can view the inspection and its review history.

If the image is dark, blurry, obstructed, or otherwise unsuitable, the report is marked Needs Review and the worker is asked to retake the photo; unresolved cases are available for supervisor review. If the API fails, the photograph remains saved and analysis is marked Failed.

## 9.9 WF-08 — After-Photo Collection and Comparison

1. Worker performs the assigned collection activity.

2. Worker captures and previews the after photograph.

3. The after photograph is uploaded and upload success is confirmed.

4. Backend requests after-photo analysis and compares it with the linked inspection when possible.

5. The worker reviews the comparison and submits the collection record.

6. The backend validates that after-photo upload succeeded before marking normal collection Completed.

7. The supervisor can review the after photo, comparison, and collection record.

If AI comparison fails, normal completion is allowed after successful photo upload and the analysis status is Failed. If the photo cannot be captured or uploaded, normal completion is blocked; a supervisor may record an Exception with a reason. An Exception is not a normal Completed collection.

## 9.10 WF-09 — AI Report Review and Correction

1. Worker opens the AI report and confirms or enters corrected values.

2. The system appends a worker review-history entry with reviewer ID and timestamp.

3. Supervisor opens the original photo and report, views worker review, and may add a supervisor correction.

4. The system appends a supervisor review-history entry.

5. Original AI data and all prior reviews remain unchanged.

## 9.11 WF-10 — Overdue Detection and Notifications

1. Supervisor configures the common inspection interval.

2. The system calculates each active bin’s next inspection due time.

3. A scheduled backend process checks due times.

4. Bins past due are identified as overdue unless a newer inspection has reset the due time.

5. The system notifies the current assigned worker/team members and the supervisor; if no current assignment exists, it notifies the supervisor.

6. Notifications are deduplicated for the same overdue event.

7. A new inspection updates the due time and closes the previous overdue event.

## 9.12 WF-11 — Dashboard and History

### Worker

1. Worker signs in and views current assigned bins in map or list view.

2. Worker reviews pending assignment responses and overdue alerts.

3. Worker opens a bin to view allowed details and history.

4. Worker reviews personal recent inspections and collection activities.

### Supervisor

1. Supervisor signs in and views all locality bins.

2. Supervisor filters by condition, assignment, priority, and overdue status.

3. Supervisor selects a bin to review latest details and complete locality history.

4. Supervisor reviews activity, emergency reports, and AI review history.

## 9.13 WF-12 — Read-Only Chatbot

1. User signs in and submits a question in Hindi or English.

2. Backend verifies identity and role.

3. Backend retrieves only records authorized for that role using approved query functions.

4. Backend supplies the question and retrieved information to Gemini.

5. Gemini returns a response grounded in the supplied information.

6. The application displays the response and handles empty results, unsupported questions, or service failures clearly.

7. Any attempted write or administrative action is refused; the chatbot cannot modify database records.

## 9.14 WF-13 — Photo Expiry

1. A scheduled backend process identifies photographs whose upload timestamp is at least 90 days old.

2. The process deletes the image object from Storage.

3. After successful deletion, the database storage path is cleared and deleted_at is recorded.

4. If deletion fails, the process preserves the path and retries later.

5. The associated operational record, AI results, and review history remain available.
